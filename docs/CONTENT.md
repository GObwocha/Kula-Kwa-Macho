# KulaKwaMacho Content Guide

## Voice

KulaKwaMacho sounds:

* clever
* playful
* warm
* Kenyan
* conversational
* confident

It should never sound like:

* a government website
* a generic restaurant chain
* a corporate SaaS platform
* an AI chatbot

---

# Naming

Use authentic food names.

Prefer:

> Nyama Choma

over:

> Kenyan Grilled Meat

Use English descriptions where useful, but preserve local food names.

---

# Food Descriptions

Descriptions should be short and appetizing.

Example:

> Charred nyama choma, smoky at the edges, juicy in the middle, and absolutely unnecessary for someone who promised to eat light today.

Avoid making unsupported historical or cultural claims.

---

# Humor

Humor should be:

* observational
* self-aware
* food-related
* occasionally Kenyan
* never hateful
* never discriminatory

Good:

> "Your rider has been stuck behind a matatu for 7 minutes."

Bad:

> stereotypes about Kenyan ethnic groups

Do not use ethnicity as the punchline.

---

# Recipe Content

Recipes must be practically useful.

Include:

* ingredients
* quantities
* preparation
* cooking
* serving suggestions

Avoid presenting uncertain nutritional or historical claims as facts.

---

# Regional Content

Kenyan food is diverse.

Use regional context when meaningful.

Potential regions:

* Coast
* Central
* Western
* Nyanza
* Rift Valley
* Eastern
* Nairobi
* Northern Kenya

Do not imply that regional categories are absolute ownership claims.

---

# CTA Examples

Possible CTAs:

* Start craving
* Surprise me
* Add to tray
* Feed the eyes
* Show me something good
* What should I eat?
* Send it
* Follow the rider
* Give me another one
* I regret nothing

Use contextually rather than everywhere.

---

# Fake Checkout Copy

Always communicate that the order is fictional.

Examples:

> "No money left your account. Your imaginary wallet is thriving."

> "Payment confirmed. In our extremely fictional economy."

> "Your order has been dispatched into the void."

---

# Delivery Copy

Examples:

> "Rider has left the kitchen."

> "Rider is negotiating traffic."

> "Rider has stopped to ask for directions."

> "Rider is approximately somewhere."

> "Almost there. Allegedly."

> "Delivered!"

> "Delivered to your imagination."

---

# Cultural Respect

Do not use:

* ethnic stereotypes
* poverty as humor
* accents as jokes
* tribal stereotypes
* fake cultural facts
* invented traditions

The humor should come from the fictional delivery experience, food cravings, internet culture, and everyday situations.
